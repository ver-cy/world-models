## Boundary hypothesis

The aggregate represents an authority-backed legal instrument and the norms expressed by addressable source fragments. It keeps legal work, expression, manifestation, source, provision and formal norm separately identifiable. Issuers, procedures, decisions, enforcement events, affected parties, generic document repositories and legal reasoning remain external.

## Required distinctions

- authoritative source versus unofficial copy, translation, summary, commentary or consolidation;
- work versus expression versus manifestation versus item;
- legal source versus formal norm and rule;
- validity versus force, efficacy, applicability and publication;
- original act versus amendment, correction and consolidated view;
- static citation versus dynamic citation;
- source-qualified conflict, exception, priority and override versus an adjudicated conclusion;
- capability, state, transition, affordance, operation, permission, constraint and failure mode.
