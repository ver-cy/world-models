## Candidate decomposition

1. Design authority and baselines: stable design/revision identity, maturity,
   representation mastership and the exact released data package.
2. Intent and justification: allocated requirements, alternatives, decisions,
   assumptions, acceptance criteria and bidirectional traceability.
3. Product definition: logical/physical architecture, component and interface
   bindings, geometry, semantic PMI, units, datums, tolerances and material.
4. Intended behavior and assurance: functional envelope, operating conditions,
   failure assumptions, analysis, design verification and design validation.
5. Change and realization: review/release authority, changes, applicability,
   deviations, manufacturing/inspection handoff and as-built reconciliation.
6. Controlled technical exchange: provenance, access, retention, package
   integrity, native/neutral conversion, conformance and explicit loss.

The design verification/validation distinction must also distinguish design
evidence from verification/validation of the manufactured end product.
Owning a design does not confer permission to manufacture, export or actuate it.
