# WM-DAT-009 verified source notes

Accessed 2026-09-06. Official standards-body, public-authority and first-party
sources were checked at canonical public pages. Provider wrappers are not sources.

- DDI Lifecycle 3.3 was published 15 April 2020. Its Data Capture guide
  distinguishes reusable QuestionItem, QuestionGrid and QuestionBlock structures
  from Instrument control constructs, order and applied use. It models response
  domains, missing values, instructions, stimuli, sequences, conditions and loops.
- DDI Common Core version 1 (2025), published in a modified form as ISO/PAS
  25955:2026, grounds concepts, variables, units and value domains shared by DDI
  products. It does not replace the richer DDI Lifecycle questionnaire structures.
- UNECE GSIM 2.0 was released in December 2023 and its User Guide in November
  2024. It separates Questionnaire Specification, components, questions,
  statements, logic and collection activities. GSBPM 5.2, CES-endorsed in June
  2025, is a statistical production-process profile, not the instrument itself.
- ISO 20252:2026 edition 4 was published 1 September 2026. Only public scope and
  lifecycle metadata are used; access-restricted requirements are not inferred.
- HL7 FHIR R5 Questionnaire 5.0.0 is a healthcare Trial Use profile. It sharply
  distinguishes Questionnaire from QuestionnaireResponse and from comparable
  clinical observations; that distinction is adopted, not the healthcare scope.
- U.S. Census Bureau 2026 guidelines, Statistics Canada quality guidance and
  current GOV.UK question guidance provide public-sector profiles for wording,
  modes, testing, branching, validation and accessibility. None is universal.
- WCAG 2.2 grounds accessible presentation. DPV 2.0 grounds privacy terms.
  PROV-O grounds derivation and attribution, SKOS controlled concepts, RFC 5646
  language tags and RFC 3339 interoperable timestamps.
- Instrument, study, campaign, wave, administration, interview, respondent,
  consent, sample, response, observation, dataset and result are separate
  identities and lifecycles. A questionnaire defines permitted responses; it
  does not contain or imply a person's actual response.
