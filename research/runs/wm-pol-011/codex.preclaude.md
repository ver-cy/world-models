## Boundary hypothesis

The aggregate represents a legally grounded tax obligation and the taxpayer or authority assessments that quantify it for a tax type, subject and period. It preserves competing calculations, decisions and balances. Taxpayers, laws, source accounting records, returns, payments, audits, collections, disputes and enforcement retain external masters.

## Required distinctions

- taxable event and potential obligation versus declared or assessed liability;
- obligation identity versus return, assessment notice, debt account and payment;
- self-assessment versus authority assessment, estimate and reassessment;
- tax base, rate, relief, credit, interest and penalty as source-qualified calculation components;
- original, amended, corrected, audited and appealed calculations;
- assessed amount versus payable balance, paid amount and collectible debt;
- procedural status versus legal effect and dispute state;
- capability, state, transition, affordance, operation, permission, constraint and failure mode.

No result may calculate universal tax, infer legal liability without a jurisdiction profile or provide tax advice.
