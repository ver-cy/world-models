# WM-ECO-032 provider focus

Research a governed Tax Return / Filing specification, not a taxpayer, tax account, tax law, calculation engine, invoice, payment, refund, assessment, audit, dispute, enforcement action or generic document master.

Resolve the slash boundary explicitly. Test one aggregate that owns the identity and versioned declaration package plus its submission and authority-response evidence, while preserving return, attachment, filing attempt, receipt, validation, acceptance, assessment, payment and refund as separate identities or state axes. A submitted return must never prove receipt, validation, acceptance, tax liability, payment, refund, compliance or finality.

Cover at minimum:

- return identity, tax type, form or schema, taxpayer and representative bindings, jurisdiction, period, filing obligation and master system;
- original, amended, corrected, superseding, supplemental, final, nil and information-return profiles;
- declaration sections, schedules, attachments, elections, claims, disclosures, source documents and supporting evidence;
- reported facts, amounts, currencies, units, tax bases, deductions, allowances, credits, rates, calculated tax and balances without absorbing calculation, ledger or payment masters;
- preparer, signer, authorized representative, filing intermediary, software provider and authority roles;
- attestation, signature, authorization, filing mandate, legal basis, consent and responsibility boundaries;
- draft, prepared, validated, signed, submitted, received, rejected, accepted-for-processing, assessed, amended, withdrawn, superseded and closed axes;
- channel, endpoint, envelope, payload, transmission, acknowledgement, receipt, validation errors, duplicate detection and idempotency;
- due date, extension, filing date, effective date, authority receipt, assessment, correction, observation, ingestion and knowledge times;
- payment, refund, offset, penalty, interest, audit, inquiry, appeal, dispute, collection and enforcement references;
- rule, form, schema, taxonomy, code list and jurisdiction versions; validation, provenance, data quality and reproducibility;
- taxpayer secrecy, personal and financial data, least privilege, disclosure, retention, legal hold, correction lineage and loss-aware projections.

Prefer current official tax-authority filing specifications, OECD exchange schemas, EU tax rules or administrative-cooperation formats, XBRL and W3C or IETF governance standards. Treat every tax type, jurisdiction, taxpayer class, period, form and filing channel as a scoped profile rather than universal law.
