# WM-MED-002 Codex pre-provider hypothesis

The useful Vercy boundary is neither “a file” nor “all facts about a creative
work”. It is an operational media-asset aggregate that lets an agent discover,
verify, choose and transform concrete technical representations without
mistaking any one storage locator or derivative for the underlying content.

A rendition must remain separately addressable because its bytes, format,
codec, dimensions, duration, tracks, language, accessibility role, digest,
quality, availability, storage placement, embargo and deletion state can all
differ from its siblings. The aggregate needs explicit relationship kinds such
as preservation master, mezzanine, access copy, thumbnail, proxy, alternate
language, caption, transcript, segment and derived output. “Original” is a
claim with provenance, not an automatically privileged filename.

Likely structural groups are: identity and boundaries; representation and
technical description; provenance and derivation; semantic and descriptive
metadata; rights, policy and authenticity; accessibility and localization;
preservation, storage and integrity; distribution, lifecycle and operations.
The canonical compact draft should merge these into roughly six coherent
bundles while keeping questions discriminating rather than repetitive.

Major semantic risks are work/asset/file collapse, checksum-as-identity,
format/container/codec confusion, ambiguous clocks, silent metadata precedence,
mutable lineage, rights inferred from possession, credentials treated as truth,
and destructive actions without authority or verifiable evidence.
