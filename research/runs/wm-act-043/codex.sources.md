# WM-ACT-043 Codex source notes

The Codex fallback used official primary material from ISO, NIST, FEMA, the
European Union, W3C and the IETF.

ISO 22301 and ISO 22313 provide the BCMS frame: governed preparation,
continuity at a predefined acceptable capacity, response, recovery and
improvement. ISO/TS 22317 supports a formal BIA process with organizational
priorities, prioritized activities, resources, dependencies, approval and
review. ISO/TS 22331 addresses strategy selection, ISO/TS 22318 supply-chain
continuity, ISO/TS 22330 people aspects and ISO 22398 exercise programmes.
Only public catalogue abstracts and publicly visible preview material were
used, so no clause-level or certification-conformance claim is made. ISO 22361
helps preserve the boundary between continuity capability and crisis command.

NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1 supplies an information-system contingency profile with
BIA, requirements, plan development, testing, training and maintenance. NIST
SP 800-184 adds recovery playbooks, tests, continual improvement and metrics.
NIST CSF 2.0 distinguishes recovery action selection and execution, backup and
restored-asset integrity checks, return of services, declaration of recovery
end and recovery communication. These technology-recovery profiles do not
define universal business continuity semantics.

FEMA's 2018 Continuity Guidance Circular with the 2024 update contributes a
United States whole-community profile for essential functions, succession,
delegation, facilities, communications, vital records, people, devolution,
exercise and reconstitution. DORA provides an EU financial-sector profile for
ICT continuity policy, BIA, recovery plans, backups, tests and records. Neither
profile is treated as universally applicable.

PROV-O grounds attributable plans, activities, evidence, revisions and
invalidations. RFC 3339 supplies timestamps with seconds and explicit offsets.

Claude Sonnet and Grok 4.6 each timed out after their single bounded 120-second
attempt, so no external result was admitted. The Plan / Schedule parent and
incoming Incident Response reference are candidate registry relations, not
approved edges. The source-grounded Codex result passed local validation and
proceeds only as a reviewable draft subject to a separate no-tools adversarial
audit.
