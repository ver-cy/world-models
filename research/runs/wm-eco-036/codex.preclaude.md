# Codex pre-Claude research: WM-ECO-036 Insurance Claim

Boundary hypothesis: one claim is an identity-bearing, versioned request and
assertion aggregate invoking an insurance promise, statute or scheme after a
loss or qualifying circumstance. It owns the claim statement, claim lines,
party roles, referenced basis, claimed facts, requested benefits and amendment
lineage. It references, but does not own, the policy, occurrence, subject,
evidence, handling work, coverage decision, reserve, settlement, payment,
complaint, dispute, fraud, recovery, regulator or records lifecycles.

Critical distinctions to test: notification versus claim presentation;
submitted versus complete or admissible; allegation versus observed fact;
claimed versus estimated, assessed, allowed, offered, settled and paid amount;
policy existence versus coverage; claim status versus handling, coverage,
payment and dispute states; duplicate versus related or successor claim; and
closure versus final legal extinguishment. Candidate containment and composition
edges remain unapproved.

Primary-source discovery set: IAIS, NAIC, FCA, EIOPA, EUR-Lex, HL7, ACORD,
ISO, IETF and W3C. External providers must verify editions and scope and must
not treat licensed ACORD material or regional rules as universal.
