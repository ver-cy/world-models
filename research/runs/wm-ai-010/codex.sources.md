# WM-AI-010 verified source notes

Accessed 2026-09-06. Official public-authority, standards-body and first-party
sources were checked at their canonical pages. Provider wrappers are not sources.

- The consolidated EU AI Act defines serious incidents and Article 73 reporting
  duties, including severity-dependent timing, incomplete initial reports,
  investigation, risk assessment and corrective action. The Commission's 2025
  Article 73 guidance and high-risk-system template remain draft at access.
- OECD publications distinguish actual-harm incidents from potential-harm
  hazards and offer a 29-criterion common reporting framework; the beta AIM is
  an evidence monitor, not an authority finding or universal incident master.
- NIST AI RMF 1.0 and AI 600-1 ground AI risk, monitoring, documentation and
  incident learning. NIST SP 800-61r3 supplies cross-organizational preparation,
  detection, response and recovery context; NIST AI 100-2e2025 supplies current
  adversarial-ML terminology.
- GDPR and final EDPB Guidelines 9/2022 provide a bounded EU personal-data-breach
  profile. An AI incident is not automatically a personal-data breach and vice versa.
- FIRST CSIRT Services Framework 2.1 describes technology-neutral incident
  management services. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is used only at the public catalogue
  metadata level because normative clauses are access restricted.
- PROV-O supports attributable derivation and revision. RFC 3339 supplies
  interoperable timestamps with seconds and explicit UTC relationship.
- The report, underlying incident, affected system, investigation, authority
  case, remediation work, evidence, submission receipt and public summary keep
  independent identities, authorities, states and times.
