The model owns a governed resource plan and its authorized revisions: budget
identity, owner, scope, period, objectives, classifications, assumptions,
scenarios, ceilings, envelopes, allocations, funding sources, approval state,
execution snapshots, forecasts, amendments, variances, controls, risks and
reports. It can specialize personal, project, organizational and public-sector
budgets through profiles. It does not own actual accounting entries,
transactions, contracts, procurement, payments, revenue collection, assets,
debts, projects, organizations, currencies, policies or official statistics.

A plan amount, appropriation, commitment, obligation, forecast, actual ledger
amount and cash payment are different records. The budget may reconcile them by
typed reference and basis mapping, but must not silently turn plan into actual.
Public-sector authority, participation and transparency rules are profiles and
must not be imposed universally on private or personal budgets.

## Conceptual structure to test

1. Budget identity, owner, purpose, scope, jurisdiction and calendar.
2. Planning horizon, financial periods, basis and currency or unit.
3. Objectives, programmes, activities, outputs, outcomes and indicators.
4. Revenue, expenditure, financing, transfer, reserve and contingency classes.
5. Baseline, economic and operational assumptions, estimates and scenarios.
6. Fiscal or resource limits, ceilings, envelopes and allocation rules.
7. Budget lines, dimensions, quantities, unit costs and funding sources.
8. Proposal, negotiation, review, authorization, appropriation and publication.
9. Commitments, obligations, encumbrances, actuals and cash by reference.
10. Forecast-to-complete, transfers, supplementary budgets and amendments.
11. Variance, explanations, corrective decisions and performance evidence.
12. Control, segregation of duties, risks, contingencies, audit and retention.
13. Transparency, participation, reporting and standards-aligned projections.

## Adversarial checks

- Do not confuse a budget with a forecast, ledger, funding agreement, purchase
  request, contract, invoice, payment or financial statement.
- Keep proposal, approved, available, committed, obligated, actual, paid and
  forecast amounts distinct and time-bound.
- Require owner, scope, period, basis, currency, classification, version,
  authority and source for every budget amount.
- Preserve original and amended versions, transfer sources and destinations,
  approval authority, effective time and justification.
- Separate budget authorization from spending authority and actual execution.
- Reconcile cash, accrual and statistical bases explicitly rather than mixing.
- Do not infer programme outcomes from expenditure or budget absorption alone.
- Keep public transparency and legislative appropriation requirements as
  jurisdictional profiles, not universal rules for all budgets.
