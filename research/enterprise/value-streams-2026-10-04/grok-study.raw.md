The minimum set is two unassigned roots: **Value Stream** (the definition) and **Value Stream Realization** (the enterprise binding). No identifier is allocated. External and internal are boundary roles of one definition, not two roots. Stages, exchange intent, and the terminal value-state are contained. Maps, portfolios, journeys, process runs, and stream “instances” do not master facts.

Archetype names below are classification labels only. They are not registry identifiers.

## A. Coverage frame

Completeness is argued on five finite dimensions, not on industries or departments.

**D1. Boundary role.** A role of one definition against one governing boundary: outbound-external (primary beneficiary outside), inbound-crossing (beneficiary inside, value or permission crosses in), or internal (beneficiary inside; the stream enables, governs, changes, or sustains). The same definition may be external to a subsidiary and internal to the group. Role change does not mint a second root.

**D2. Value-cycle phase.** Every admitted stream must show a beneficiary value-state progression in at least one of: acquisition, creation, delivery, realization, capture, renewal/retirement, change, enablement, governance, assurance, recovery. A phase is not itself a stream. A phase is covered if it is the terminal phase of some archetype or a real stage of one.

**D3. Beneficiary class.** One primary class per stream: external user, right-holder, duty-subject, funded performer, partner, network participant, relying party, or harmed external party; the enterprise as recipient of supply, return, resources, or authorization; an internal operation; a worker; a governing body; a decision-maker; a risk owner; or the enterprise as duty-bearer. Two beneficiary classes means two streams and a hand-off.

**D4. Terminal-outcome class.** The class of beneficiary condition that ends the stream, stated separately from outputs, delivery, payment, and accounting recognition.

**D5. Exclusions.** Department lists, generic value-chain templates, application workflows, project plans, touchpoint-only journeys, and product or asset lifecycles with no beneficiary value progression. Also excluded: seller pipeline states, system status, report publication, course delivery, go-live, and ticket closure, unless a beneficiary state sequence remains after those outputs are removed.

**Admission rule.** An archetype is a class-level stream: named beneficiary class, recognizable trigger, ordered beneficiary states, terminal outcome distinct from outputs, identity stable across process, system, and organization redesign. A variant may change channel, sector, offering form, jurisdiction, or price. It may not change beneficiary class, trigger class, state-sequence meaning, or terminal-outcome class. If one of those four changes, it is another definition or a new semantic version, not a variant.

**Coverage claim.** Any admissible stream lands on exactly one archetype by beneficiary class, trigger class, and terminal-outcome class. Every phase in D2 is terminal for at least one archetype below, or a non-terminal stage of one. A new sector produces variants. A new archetype is justified only by a new beneficiary class or terminal-outcome class, and that extension must be explicit.

Renewal is not an archetype. It is a new cycle of the same definition when the trigger is “continuation required.” Retirement is either Active holding to closed holding or the closure stage of the worker, asset, or obligation streams.

## D. Composition rules

**Nesting.** A definition contains ordered stages and nothing that has a different beneficiary. A stage is a value-state transition: entry condition, exit condition, same beneficiary. If a supposed stage has its own beneficiary, it is another stream.

**Decomposition.** Further breakdown of work inside a stage is process, procedure, or activity (WM-ACT-003, WM-ACT-009, EM-OPS-01). It must not be stored as child streams. A shared library of stage names fails transfer: the same label under different beneficiaries is not the same value state. Shared names, if needed, are classifications over WM-KNW-018, not a root.

**Variants.** Variants specialize channel, sector, offering, place, or commercial form. They inherit beneficiary, trigger, state meaning, and terminal class. Semantic version of a definition changes only when beneficiary class, trigger class, state sequence or state meaning, or terminal outcome changes. Label-only edits are history (WM-XCT-022 pattern), not a new semantic version. Process, system, organization, metric, and channel changes do not version the definition.

**Cross-boundary hand-off.** A hand-off links the exit state of one stream to the trigger of another. It is not containment. Accepted supply may enable an offering stream; a releasable offering may enable Demand to realized use; a claim arising on that stream may trigger Earned claim to secured return before or after use is realized. Order is not a universal sequence. External versus internal is reclassified per boundary (WM-ORG-001, EM-ORG-01, EM-LND-02). It is not copied into a second definition.

**Many-to-many realization.** One stage may be realized by many processes, capabilities, units, or systems. One process may realize stages of many streams. Those links are not inside the definition. If they were, a redesign would create a new stream version and contradict the identity already stated for Value Stream: the progression survives process redesign, automation, and organization change. Missing realizers are gaps. They are not evidence that the stream is absent or that realization failed.

**Not a process.** A process masters how work is executed. A stream masters how a beneficiary’s value state is intended to change. Execution records do not create stream identity.

**Not a journey.** A journey is a touchpoint or trip sequence (WM-FLW-009; EM-TEC-06 deferred and non-normative). Touchpoints become stages only when they change beneficiary value state.

**Not a lifecycle.** Product, asset, employment, and contract lifecycles master the subject’s states. They are streams only where a named beneficiary’s value progresses. Otherwise they remain WM-OBJ-022, WM-ORG-005, WM-ECO-006, and peers.

**Not a capability.** A capability is an ability identified independently of organization and process (EM-STR-03 pair of outcome class and acted-upon object). A stream may be enabled by capabilities. It is not a capability and does not own Capability Realization.

**Not a value chain.** A generic chain is a fixed activity classification with no beneficiary, trigger, or terminal outcome. It may inspire variants. It is not a definition.

**Not an instance.** One customer’s passage, one hire, or one incident is a correlation across existing occurrence masters. Independent instance identity is not demonstrated. Correlation keys are views, as with the rejected independent identities of Backlog and Iteration.

## F. Minimum new roots

### Value Stream — passes

- **Identity.** One governed recipient-value progression: primary beneficiary class, triggering need, ordered beneficiary value states, and one terminal outcome distinct from outputs. The frozen statement holds: it remains identifiable across process redesign, automation, and organization change.
- **Lifecycle.** Authored, reviewed, effective, superseded, or retired, independently of realizing processes, projects, systems, and reporting periods.
- **Mastership.** One definition owner under the EM-STR-01 contour. Strategy may select it. A business model may use it. A process may realize it. None of those is the master.
- **Version driver.** Semantic version changes only if beneficiary class, trigger class, state sequence or state meaning, or terminal outcome changes.
- **Transfer.** Another legal entity may adopt the same definition. Stewardship may move without changing identity. Redesign of work does not.
- **Aggregate.** Beneficiary specification; trigger; contained stages; contained terminal condition; optional contained exchange intent; archetype classification. Not contained: metrics, processes, systems, maps, benefits, cash, outcomes-as-catalogue.
- **Invariants.** Exactly one primary beneficiary class. At least an entry state and a terminal state. Each stage’s exit is the next stage’s entry. A stage is not an internal work step. External/internal is not intrinsic. Realization links are forbidden inside the definition. Absence of evidence is unknown, not failure.

### Value Stream Realization — passes, and is separate because ownership and version driver differ

- **Identity.** One governing boundary’s binding of exactly one immutable Value Stream version, plus a scope (enterprise-wide, or a referenced offering, unit, region, or population). Tool and diagram changes do not change it.
- **Lifecycle.** Proposed, active, superseded when the operating binding changes, while the definition remains.
- **Mastership.** The operation accountable for the operating model, not the definition owner.
- **Version driver.** Realizer set, stage-to-realizer mapping, scope, or effective interval. It must not change beneficiary, trigger, states, or terminal outcome. If those change, this object points at a new definition version instead.
- **Transfer.** Stewardship can move inside the same boundary and remain the same fact. Another enterprise adopting the definition gets a new realization. That is the correct result of the transfer test, not a failure.
- **Aggregate.** Pinned definition version; governing organization; scope references; contained stage bindings to pinned realizers; explicit gaps; stewardship reference. No restated value states.
- **Invariants.** Cannot “correct” a definition by local stages. Zero realizers on a stage is a gap, not a negative outcome. Many realizers per stage are allowed. The same realizer may serve many realizations.

**Near-misses that fail and are not roots.**

- Stage library: fails transfer once removed from its stream.
- Stream instance: occurrence facts already have masters; correlation is a view.
- Map, portfolio, landscape: views do not mint business identity; EM-LND-04 and EM-LND-11 stay projections.
- Value Exchange: no residue left after contained intent plus existing commercial masters.
- Outcome and Benefit: deferred or rejected by EM-STR-02; identity statement for the boundary register is absent.
- Value Proposition: already contained; it has no state progression.
- Separate external and internal roots: forbidden by the role rule.
- Profiling realization onto WM-XCT-037: impact dependency and operating realization are different facts. EM-WRK-06 already uses that dependency family for impact.

## G. Negative cases

1. **Order-to-cash.** Commercial process across WM-ECO-020, WM-ECO-008, WM-ECO-009, and WM-ECO-016. Invoice and cash are outputs. Customer value is Demand to realized use. Secured return is the capture stream. Payment proves neither outcome nor benefit.
2. **Hire-to-retire.** An HR process family across vacancy, employment, learning, payroll, and separation. It mixes enterprise benefit with worker benefit. Admissible pieces are Unmet contribution need to reliable contribution and Worker expectation to honored relationship, and only as value states, not as ATS steps.
3. **Incident-to-recovery.** WM-ACT-019, WM-ACT-042, and WM-ACT-021 workflow. Ticket closure is an output. The streams are restored external value or a restored internal baseline.
4. **Strategy-to-outcome.** A management chain across strategy, initiative, portfolio, project, and KPI. It has no single beneficiary progression. EM-STR-01 Strategy remains a choice set. Direction and operating-state streams cover only their own terminals. Benefit realization stays deferred.
5. **Data-to-insight.** Pipelines, studies, and reports (WM-DAT-005, WM-ACT-036, WM-REC-002). A published report is an output. Decision question to accepted understanding applies only when a named decision-maker’s accepted understanding is the terminal state. Otherwise it is not a stream.
6. **Procure-to-pay.** Requisition through payment. Accepted supply ends when fit input is accepted. Paying the supplier is settlement of the supplier’s claim, not proof that supply was fit.
7. **Lead-to-opportunity.** Seller states on WM-ECO-026 and WM-ECO-027. Awareness and pipeline stage are not beneficiary value states.
8. **Idea-to-product lifecycle.** Roadmap, design, and production run. Offering gap to releasable offering is admissible only when “releasable to a beneficiary” is the terminal state. Design approval alone is not.
9. **Record-to-report.** Close, consolidation, and statement issue (EM-FIN-04, WM-ECO-018). A statement is an output. Consolidation Scope and Run remain execution identities, not streams.
10. **Plan-to-deliver.** Project phases, milestones, and gate reviews. WM-ACT-005 completion does not mean an operating state is in force or that a beneficiary outcome occurred.
11. **Customer-journey map.** Touchpoints and channels. EM-TEC-06 User Journey stays deferred and non-normative. It does not become a stream by being drawn under the stream’s name.
12. **Application workflow.** Status machines inside WM-SFT-002 or case tools. System state is not beneficiary state.
13. **Generic value chain.** A fixed list of primary and support activities. No beneficiary, trigger, or terminal outcome. Excluded even if every department is mapped onto it.
14. **Risk-register-to-heatmap.** Maintenance of WM-KNW-015 rows and a view. An authorized risk position is a stream only when the owner’s position is the terminal state. The heatmap is a projection.
15. **Deploy-to-operate.** Build, release, and deployment (WM-SFT-008, WM-SFT-009). Go-live is an output. It realizes Ability gap to reliable ability only if reliability of the ability is the terminal state.
16. **Cost-to-serve.** A metric view over delivery, using EM-FIN-05. Cost is neither the beneficiary outcome nor value capture.
