## Lifecycle

CTL-03 requires an effective control to reference "exactly one immutable approved revision" while the revision vocabulary separates `approved` from `effective`, leaving CTL-03's referent ambiguous.

The effect of control-identity supersession on dependent contexts is undefined.

The `revision` key's ordering and immutability are undeclared, and CTL-16 protects control identifiers from recycling but not revision identifiers.
12. **Operating state is missing though asserted.** CTL-05 distinguishes operation from design, implementation, execution and effectiveness, but no operation plane exists (operating / suspended / not operating, with expected versus observed occurrences by reference), and `suspended` does double duty as a control-lifecycle state.
13. **Forward reliance on dead revisions is unconstrained.** CTL-13 marks dependent contexts stale after supersession or retirement; no rule bars a new reliance citation on a withdrawn, retired or superseded revision.

23. **Upstream risk-side staleness has no mechanism.** CTL-13 covers the control side; a superseded upstream risk revision marking the binding stale is neither a rule nor an explicit hold, though the material names it a declared gap.
24. **"Evidence chain" is load-bearing and undefined.** CTL-06 and CTL-12 turn on it, yet no rule requires an evidence reference to resolve with a matching digest, states that non-resolution or digest mismatch invalidates the determination rather than degrading it silently, or separates lawful retention and destruction from falsification.

27. **Acceptance semantics are thin.** EP-06 and EP-09 make acceptance external and non-implied, but no rule requires a referenced decision with sufficient recorded authority, threshold-table version and authorisation time, and none states that acceptance does not close a risk.
