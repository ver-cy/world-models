Which identifier is authoritative for this item, who issued it, under which scheme and syntax, and what uniqueness guarantees apply. non-serial-identification

Legibility, cloning risk and distinguishing marks

Object ID requires inscriptions and markings, distinguishing features and photographs because unique numbers can be copied, lost or never assigned. A cloned serial or unreadable tag does not by itself prove a second item, nor does it destroy the original item's identity. Condition of the mark is evidence about identification quality, not a new identifier.

    • assessor_ref
    • method
  2. What Object ID-style distinguishing features, inscriptions or damage patterns uniquely describe this object even if serials are absent or cloned?identity
    Expected answer
    • inscriptions
    • distinguishing_features
    • feature_photographs[]
    • measurement_set_ref
  3. Is there evidence that a presented identifier is cloned, counterfeit, reused or bound to another item, and what is the confidence and evidence of that suspicion?security
    Expected answer
    • suspicion_status (none|suspect|confirmed-conflict)
    • conflicting_item_id
    • evidence_refs[]
    • confidence

digital-representation-binding

Digital link, passport and shell

IEC 61406-1 defines the IL string as a URL that is both the globally unique identification of a physical object and a pointer to related information. Schema.org requires that a GS1 Digital Link containing serial AI 21 appear only on IndividualProduct. ESPR establishes a digital product passport with a unique product identifier, unique operator identifier and unique facility identifier, accessed via a data carrier on the product. IDTA AAS gives the asset a recommended globalAssetId (IEC 61406 format) and optional specificAssetIds, while the Administration Shell has a separate mandatory id. These are representations and links, not a second physical item.

Questions
  1. What resolvable IRI or URL currently identifies this physical object (IEC 61406 IL, GS1 Digital Link with AI 21, DPP unique product identifier), and does it encode instance grain rather than type or lot?identity
    Expected answer
    • resolvable_iris[] with scheme, url, grain (instance|type|lot|unspecified), last_resolved_at, resolution_status.
  2. Which digital representations (DPP, AAS, other twin) describe this item, what are their own identifiers, and which of those identifiers are explicitly not the item master?interoperability
    Expected answer
    • representations[] with kind (dpp|aas|twin|other), representation_id, global_asset_id, specific_asset_ids, distinct_from_item_master (boolean).
  3. If a DPP or similar passport exists, what unique operator identifier and unique facility identifier are bound to this item instance, and at which production event?provenance
    Expected answer
    • operator_identifier
    • facility_identifier
    • bound_at_event_id
    • event_time
    • Treat operator and facility as referenced parties/places, not as item identity.
Artifacts
  • Digital representation bindingA link from the physical item to an IL, Digital Link, DPP or AAS record, recording that the representation id is not a second item master.

configuration-and-lot-linkage

Configuration and lot inputs

A product configuration may classify a configured instance (variant-instance boundary). A lot or batch identifier is not an instance identifier; Schema.org places lot-bearing Digital Links on SomeProducts unless there is only a specific product. IEC 61406-2 separately treats lots/batches. Lot membership and configuration are classification or provenance facts on the instance.

loss-theft-and-recovery

Loss, theft, seizure, recovery and deaccession

Object ID exists because stolen and trafficked objects cannot be recovered without documentation, photographs and distinguishing features; INTERPOL and customs use that documentation. Unknown whereabouts is not the same as non-existence. Seizure, loan, exhibition and deaccession change custody or collection membership without necessarily destroying the object. These states lack a single global code list and must be recorded as explicit exceptional statuses with evidence.

    Expected answer

    • asserted_at
    • asserted_by
    • legal_reference
    • last_known_location_ref
    • last_known_custodian_ref
  2. If previously missing or stolen, when was it recovered or returned, by whom, and did identity, condition or custody need re-validation?event
    Expected answer
    • recovery_event_time
    • recovered_by
    • identity_revalidated (boolean)
    • condition_assessment_id
    • new_custody_record_id
  3. Has a museum, court or owner deaccessioned, forfeited or legally abandoned the item, and does that end title, custody, or only collection membership?ownership
    Expected answer
    • deaccession_or_forfeiture (boolean)
    • instrument_ref
    • effects_on (title|custody|collection-membership)
    • event_time

Publication holds

  • Source verification hold: every accepted source URL, organisation, version pin and access date must be re-fetched and confirmed live before publication, including the two providers' divergent GS1 endpoints (ref.gs1.org/epcis/ versus ref.gs1.org/standards/epcis/ and /standards/cbv/) and the two IEC 61406-1 webstore URLs.
  • Multi-profile validation hold: the merged structure has not been exercised against distinct domain profiles - serialised industrial equipment, consumer trade items, cultural and museum objects, unmarked natural specimens, and logistics handling-unit contexts - so profile-specific mandatory elements may still be missing.
  • Paywalled clause text hold: ISO 55000:2024, ISO 55001:2024, ISO 22095:2020 and ISO/IEC 15459-4 were grounded from catalogue and overview metadata only in both runs; clause-level obligations on asset lifecycle, chain-of-custody models and unique item identifiers remain unverified.
  • ESPR/DPP grain hold: both providers failed to render the EUR-Lex text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, so whether the unique product identifier binds at model, batch or item level, and the registry and data-carrier duties, rest on secondary summaries and must not be published as article-level facts.
  • EPCIS and CBV normative text hold: the base grounded EPCIS/CBV fields from the GS1 linked-data reference corroborated by a tier-3 implementation site; CBV vocabulary claims carried by the accepted event-dimensions finding must be reconciled against the ratified EPCIS 2.0 and CBV 2.0 standards before publication.
  • Local-policy labelling hold: identifier precedence across GS1, ISO/IEC 15459, IEC 61406 and AAS namespaces, and the identity-continuity rule across component replacement and rebuild, are unresolved by every consulted source and must be published as explicitly local decisions rather than standards-backed facts.

Deferred research

  • Sector-specific instance identification regimes omitted by both providers - ISO 3779 VIN, medical device UDI, IMEI, aerospace and defence item unique identification, and firearms marking - which may impose mandatory identity elements on the instance-identification layer.
  • ISO/IEC 15459 series clause text and GS1 General Specifications 8.0.0 provisions on issuing agencies, non-reuse periods and identifier retirement, to decide whether identifier issuance and post-disposal embargo deserves its own finding beside master-identifier.
  • EPCIS TransformationEvent and PROV wasDerivedFrom grounding for the base identity-continuity finding, plus CBV completeness_verified versus completeness_inferred for containment-context, as evidence enrichment rather than new nodes.
  • ICOM Object ID descriptive categories (title, subject, date or period, maker) and museum condition-report taxonomies, to test whether cultural descriptive identity is a distinct facet of the observed-properties layer or remains covered by measured-attributes and origin-provenance.
  • Darwin Core and biological collecting-event practice for natural specimens taken into tracking, since natural-object admission currently rests only on a broad CIDOC E18 alignment.
  • Automatic data capture standards (RFID air interfaces, 2D symbol quality grading) and metrology vocabulary for measurement uncertainty, both unconsulted in either run.
  • Deaccession, forfeiture, salvage and waste-shipment regimes (including Basel Convention) that govern the accepted exceptional-states and end-of-life findings in jurisdictions beyond the EU assumptions already recorded.
