Observation / Measurement Record

Provide a format-neutral context structure for a single observation act and the measurement record it produces, binding a feature of interest, an observable property, an observing procedure, a result value and its unit, together with time, uncertainty, quality, provenance and governance context, so that an AI agent can create, inspect, validate and operate observation records without owning the lifecycles of the sensors, samples, methods or catalogues it references.

provenance-chain

Provenance chain and derivation

How the record states what generated it, which prior entities it used or was derived from, and how the original acquisition record is preserved so that the reported value can be traced back to what the instrument or observer actually produced.

Questions
  1. Which activity generated this record, and what did that activity use as input?provenance
    Expected answer
    • Generating activity reference and kind
    • Input entity references
    • Activity start and end instants with explicit offsets
  2. Which prior observations or entities was this result derived from, and by what transformation?relationship
    Expected answer
    • Source observation or entity references
    • Transformation or algorithm reference and version
    • Whether the derivation is reversible or lossy
  3. Where is the original acquisition record, and how is the reported value shown to correspond to it?evidence
    Expected answer
    • Original record reference and digest
    • Correspondence statement between original and reported value
    • Any processing applied between the two
  4. Has this record been reprocessed, and which processing version produced the current value?lifecycle
    Expected answer
    • Processing version identifier
    • Prior processing versions and their outputs
    • Reason for reprocessing
Artifacts
  • Original acquisition recordThe original record captured at the moment of acquisition, in the form in which the observer or instrument produced it, retained unaltered so that the reported result can be traced back to it. Corrections never overwrite it; they create a new record version that references it.
ownership-and-attribution

Ownership, attribution and responsible agents

Who owns the record, who is attributed as its producer, which organisation is the authoritative source for which part of it, and who may be contacted about it.

Questions
  1. Which organisation owns this record and is accountable for its correctness?ownership
    Expected answer
    • Owning organisation reference
    • Accountability statement
    • Effective date of ownership and any transfer history
  2. To whom is the observation attributed, and does that differ from the owner?ownership
    Expected answer
    • Attributed agent references and roles
    • Difference statement between owner and producer
    • Delegation relationship where an agent acted on behalf of another
  3. Which organisation is the authoritative source for each part of the record when several contribute?authority
    Expected answer
    • Part-to-authority mapping
    • Conflict resolution rule between contributors
    • Effective interval per authority
  4. Who is the responsible contact for questions about this observation, and how is that contact kept current?process
    Expected answer
    • Contact reference
    • Contact role
    • Owner of contact-record maintenance
    • Staleness handling rule

access-classification

Access classification and disclosure constraints

How the record declares its sensitivity, the licence or access rights under which it may be used, and any constraint arising from personal, commercial or security sensitivity of the observation or its subject.

Questions
  1. What sensitivity classification applies to this record and to its result payload separately?security
    Expected answer
    • Record classification code
    • Payload classification where different
    • Classification scheme reference
    • Classifying authority
  2. Under which access rights and licence may this observation be used and redistributed?access
    Expected answer
    • Access rights statement
    • Licence identifier
    • Attribution requirement
    • Redistribution constraint
  3. Does the observation identify or relate to an identifiable person, and what constraint follows?privacy
    Expected answer
    • Personal-data indicator
    • Basis of the determination
    • Applicable protection regime reference
    • Owner of the lawful-basis decision
  4. Is the record embargoed or restricted for a period, and what releases it?exception
    Expected answer
    • Embargo indicator and expiry with explicit offset
    • Releasing condition or authority
    • Behaviour of derived products during embargo
retention-and-disposition

Retention, disposition and tombstoning

How long the record and its payload must be kept, which policy determines that, what disposition decision was reached, and what evidence remains after the record is destroyed.

Questions
  1. Which retention policy governs this record, and what retention period does it impose?retention
    Expected answer
    • Retention policy reference and version
    • Retention period or trigger
    • Start event for the retention clock
    • Owner of the policy
  2. Do the record, its result payload and its original acquisition record carry different retention periods?retention
    Expected answer
    • Per-component retention periods
    • Justification for divergence
    • Rule preventing payload loss while the record is retained
  3. Is the record under legal hold or another suspension of disposition, and who imposed it?authority
    Expected answer
    • Hold indicator
    • Imposing authority reference
    • Hold scope and expected review date
  4. What tombstone remains after disposition, and what may it disclose?lifecycle
    Expected answer
    • Tombstone content specification (identifier, disposition instant, policy reference, authority)
    • Fields that must not survive disposition
    • Resolution behaviour for references to the destroyed record
interoperability-and-membershipInteroperability and Membership3 findings

Alignment to external observation standards, resolution of identifiers across systems, and membership in collections and series.

standard-alignment-and-encoding

Standard alignment and encoding binding

Which external observation standards this record is aligned to, which elements map cleanly, where mappings are lossy, and how the record is projected into a named exchange encoding without the encoding becoming the semantics.

Questions
  1. Which external observation standards and versions is this record aligned to, and is the alignment tested or asserted?interoperability
    Expected answer
    • Standard identifiers and versions
    • Alignment scope per standard
    • Tested or asserted marker
    • Evidence reference where tested
  2. Which elements are lost, coerced or fabricated when projecting into each target encoding?constraint
    Expected answer
    • Element-level mapping table with loss annotations
    • Fabrication risks (for example inferred per-observation units)
    • Round-trip fidelity verdict
  3. Where do two aligned standards disagree about the same concept, and which is authoritative for this record?decision
    Expected answer
    • Conflicting concept pairs
    • Disagreement description
    • Authoritative choice and its rationale
  4. Which encoding and profile was used for a given exchanged representation of this record?provenance
    Expected answer
    • Encoding name and version
    • Profile or schema reference
    • Producing agent
    • Instant of production with explicit offset
Artifacts
  • Encoded observation representationA concrete exchanged representation of the observation record in a named standard encoding and profile. It is a projection of the record, never its source of truth: it carries the encoding name, profile reference, producing agent and production instant so that a received representation can be traced back to the record state it was made from.
identifier-resolution

Identifier resolution and cross-system correlation

How identifiers on and around this record resolve to the things they name, how the same observation is correlated across systems that assign their own keys, and how duplicate or conflicting records are detected and reconciled.

Questions
  1. How does a consumer resolve each identifier on this record to the entity it names?interoperability
    Expected answer
    • Resolution mechanism per identifier scheme
    • Whether the identifier is resolvable or opaque
    • Fallback when resolution fails
  2. How is the same observation correlated when two systems have each assigned their own key?relationship
    Expected answer
    • Correlation key or matching rule
    • Confidence of the correlation
    • Reference to the accepted master identifier
  3. What constitutes a duplicate observation, and how is a duplicate reconciled rather than silently merged?validation
    Expected answer
    • Duplicate-detection rule
    • Fields that must match
    • Reconciliation outcome record
    • Prohibition on silent merge
  4. What happens when a referenced entity has been superseded, merged or destroyed since the observation was made?exception
    Expected answer
    • Stale-reference handling rule
    • Whether the historical reference is preserved as recorded
    • Redirection or tombstone behaviour of the target
collection-and-series-membership

Collection, datastream and series membership

How a single observation declares its membership in the collections, datastreams, series or composite observations that contain it, and which properties it inherits from them, without absorbing the containing aggregate's identity or completeness accounting.

Questions
  1. Which collection, datastream, series or dataset does this observation belong to?composition
    Expected answer
    • Container reference and kind
    • Container-owning model
    • Position or ordering within the container where significant
  2. Which properties does this observation inherit from its container, and which does it override?composition
    Expected answer
    • Inherited property list with effective values
    • Overridden properties
    • Rule for resolving inheritance conflicts
  3. Is this observation part of a composite or grouped observation, and can it be interpreted on its own?relationship
    Expected answer
    • Parent or group observation reference
    • Sibling member references
    • Standalone-interpretability verdict
  4. If extracted from its container, does the record still carry everything needed to interpret the value?validation
    Expected answer
    • Completeness verdict for standalone use
    • Missing inherited elements
    • Materialisation rule for extraction

Publication holds

  • Single-provider hold: Grok was waived by the repository owner on 2026-08-29 after repeated structured-output failures, so no independent second-provider review exists. The registry provenance line also credits a prior Claude adversarial audit, and this audit is likewise Claude, so every adversarial pass on this model is same-provider. The draft must carry this on its face and must not be read as cross-provider corroborated.
  • Live source and version verification hold: all sixteen source URLs, retrieval dates and version pins must be re-verified before publication, with specific attention to SRC-001 OMS 3.0 / OGC 20-082r4 / ISO 19156:2023, SRC-003 SOSA/SSN 2023 Edition, the SRC-008 JCGM set including the claimed JCGM 100:2008 Amd.1:2026, SRC-009 UCUM 2.2, SRC-010 QUDT 2.1 and the SRC-002 raw GitHub draft catalogue dated 2021-08-20.
  • Cardinality hold: the published normative OMS/ISO 19156:2023 requirements classes were never read. Every required-element and multiplicity claim rests on a draft working catalogue marked draft and dated 2021-08-20, and the result itself records a conflict between that catalogue's 0..* properties and the tighter cardinalities implied by SensorThings and FHIR. The model may not leave candidate status until each cardinality is re-verified against the published text.
  • Relationship-contract hold: the frozen contract is empty while the result asserts eleven owning neighbours and describes REFERENCE links to sibling models that are not registered. The draft must state that the ownership boundary is currently narrative and unenforced, and no downstream model may rely on those links until the contract is populated.
  • Artifact-identity hold: the encoded-observation-representation identity strategy cannot distinguish repeated projections of the same record version by different agents, the result-payload-object identity contradicts the model's own rule that a locator or file name is never an identifier, and the original-acquisition-record is many-to-one against the observations that claim it. These three defects must be corrected or explicitly disclosed in the published draft.
  • Entry-kind hold: the frozen registry carries entry_kind standalone-mm, which classifies the record plane only. Publication must state the subject-model kind as event alongside the record-plane value, so that no consumer treats standalone-mm as a semantic classification of the observation itself.
  • Independent second-provider review was explicitly waived by the repository owner; this Claude-only result remains a reviewable draft.
